Mission

A model can only classify a person as well as the measurements it takes as input. Real-world data are messy: items can be biased, data can be missing, and models can miss necessary complexity. When that happens, methods lose accuracy, and the people most likely to be misclassified are often those already underserved. The CARAT lab develops methods that address these limits by connecting psychometrics and machine learning. Much of our applied work centers on misophonia, where reliable measurement is still being built.

What CARAT stands for

C

Classification

Building and comparing classifiers for psychological data, and asking when a machine learning model beats a traditional psychometric one.

A

Assessment

Evaluating and sharpening measurement instruments, from full-length scales to efficient short forms that keep their precision.

R

Replication

Asking whether findings hold up, with a focus on the replication of measurement, and modeling good open-science practice.

A

Algorithms

Random forests, CART, regularized regression, and genetic algorithms for classification and variable selection under realistic conditions.

T

Testing

Using Monte Carlo simulation to stress-test methods under realistic data conditions and make practical recommendations to applied researchers.

What we are working on

Classification and item bias

Diagnostic accuracy under unmodeled DIF

Comparing IRT and random forest classification when differential item functioning cannot be directly modeled, now extending to multidimensional assessments.

Algorithms and missing data

Variable selection when data are incomplete

Benchmarking LASSO, elastic net, and genetic-algorithm selection across missing-data mechanisms, with reproducible tutorials in R.

Assessment and misophonia

Measuring and characterizing misophonia

Psychometric evaluation of the Duke Misophonia Questionnaire, a short form, and a latent profile analysis of symptom subtypes.
